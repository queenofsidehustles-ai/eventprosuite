Module 6 — Business Skills

How to Market Your Printables

You have the store. You have the templates. Now let's get customers. This guide walks you through exactly how to market your digital printables business — step by step, with real scripts and strategies you can use today.

1
Know Exactly Who You're Selling To
▼

Before you post a single thing, you need to know who your customer is. Party printables sell to parents, mostly moms, planning kids' birthday parties. They want something cute, personalized, and fast. They do not want to spend hours designing — they want to download, print, and be done.

Your Target Customer in One Sentence
A mom planning her child's 5th birthday party who needs chip bags, water bottle labels, and cupcake toppers by Friday, doesn't know Canva, and is willing to pay $15–25 to not have to figure it out herself.
What She's Searching For
• "Unicorn birthday party chip bags"
• "Spiderman birthday printable bundle"
• "Princess party labels Canva template"
• "Birthday chip bags near me" (she wants it now)
• "Editable party printables download"

4
Content That Creates Sales (Photos + Videos)
▼

You don't need a ring light or a professional camera. The best-performing content for party printables is simple, real, and relatable.

Top 5 Content Ideas (Start Here)
1. Flat lay photo: Print the chip bags, lay them flat with party decorations around them. Take a photo from above. Caption: "New [theme] bundle is live!"

2. Before/after: Plain chip bag → branded chip bag. Takes 10 seconds in Canva. Shows how easy it is.

3. "Party prep" video: Film yourself setting up a party table with your printables displayed. 30 seconds. No talking needed.

4. Customer results: If someone bought and sent a photo, share it (with permission). Social proof sells.

5. Price breakdown: "This chip bag printable costs $4. You get 12 bags. That's $0.33 per bag vs. $3+ at a party store." Simple math = instant value.
Photo Tips (No Camera Skills Needed)
• Use natural light (next to a window)
• Add a white foam board as a background — $2 at Dollar Tree
• Use a balloon and a tablecloth to add color to the scene
• iPhone photos are completely fine — don't overthink it

5
Pricing Your Printables to Actually Sell
▼

Pricing wrong is one of the fastest ways to kill your sales. Too cheap looks low quality. Too expensive loses first-time buyers. Here's the sweet spot.

Recommended Pricing (Proven Price Points)
• Individual item (1 design — e.g., chip bag only): $4–$7
• Mini bundle (3–4 items): $9–$12
• Full party bundle (8–11 items): $15–$22
• Custom order (you edit for them): $25–$35

Start at the middle of each range. You can raise prices once you have reviews.
The Custom Order Upsell
Offer to edit the template for customers who don't want to do it themselves. Charge $10–$15 extra. This requires no extra product — just 15 minutes in Canva. High-margin, high-value. Mention it in your posts: "Don't want to edit it yourself? I'll do it for you for $15 — DM me!"
6
Your First 10 Customers — The Fast Track Plan
▼

Your first 10 customers are the hardest — and the most important. Once you have 10, you have proof, you have reviews (hopefully), and your confidence is through the roof. Here's the exact plan.

Days 1–3: Tell Your Network
Text 20 people you know and say: "Hey! I just launched a digital printables shop for kids' birthday parties. Would you be willing to share my store link? Here it is: [LINK]"

Post on your personal Facebook page: "I just started something new! Party printables for kids — fully customized chip bags, labels, toppers and more. Here's my shop: [LINK] 🎉"
Days 4–7: Post in 5 Facebook Groups Daily
Find mom groups, local community groups, and party planning groups. Post a value-first message (not a sales pitch). Rotate the scripts from Module 3 so you're not posting the same thing twice.
Launch Week Offer (Optional but Powerful)
For your first week only, offer 20% off. Put it in your post: "Grand opening week — 20% off all bundles! Use code WELCOME at checkout." This creates urgency without devaluing your work permanently.
7
Making It Repeat — How to Get Customers to Come Back
▼

A customer who comes back once is worth 3x a new customer. Repeat business is where the real money is. Here's how to make it happen automatically.

After Every Sale
Send a follow-up message (if you have their contact): "Thanks so much for your order! How did the party go? I'd love to see a photo if you have one 🎉 And whenever the next party is coming up, I've got tons more themes ready for you!"
Seasonal Planning Posts
Post ahead of high-party seasons: Back-to-School August, Halloween October, Christmas December, Spring/Easter March–April. Say: "Party season is coming! Get your printables ready now — [theme] bundles are live in my shop."
The Sibling Reminder
Most families have multiple kids. After a sale, say: "When the next birthday rolls around for a sibling or friend, come back! I'm adding new themes every month."
